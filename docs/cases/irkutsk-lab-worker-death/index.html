Irkutsk laboratory-worker death: reported event, cause not established.

Public reporting about “Russia plague” is separated here from what health authorities and the World Health Organization had actually established at the review cutoff.

Current bounded state: DEATH REPORTED · CAUSE NOT ESTABLISHED · REVIEW INCOMPLETE. A worker death and an official description of pneumonia of unknown origin are reported. A causative agent, pneumonic plague diagnosis, occupational pathogen link, laboratory accident, second plague case and second death are not established by the qualifying public record reviewed here.

What the record supports

A death was reported; the diagnostic boundary remains open.

The WHO-linked UN newsroom report and later reporting say a woman who worked at the institute died. Russian authorities described pneumonia of unknown origin. WHO said the causative agent was not yet known.

SUPPORTED

Worker and death

The deceased woman was publicly identified by role as a laboratory worker at the institute, and her death was reported.

SUPPORTED AS REPORTED

Pneumonia of unknown origin

This is the attributed official description, not a final pathogen diagnosis.

NOT ESTABLISHED

Pneumonic plague

General facts about plague do not diagnose this event. The event-specific causative agent remained unknown at the review cutoff.

Source boundary

Direct public-health attribution is separated from repetition.

Multiple articles can depend on the same authority statement and are not counted as independent medical confirmation.

Event-specific sources

WHO-linked and attributed reporting

UN Geneva / WHO statement · AP update · AP initial report · TASS report attributing Rospotrebnadzor · DW fact check.

Context only

Medical and institutional background

WHO plague fact sheet explains that plague is a bacterial disease; it does not identify this event's cause. The institute page describes its remit but does not prove an exposure.

Added procedure and risk source

WHO follow-up, 7 October

WHO original remarks support the information and verification requests, and the limit on a full risk assessment. The attributed public post relays a contact-observation summary. The historical Reuters release summary could not be reopened in this repair.

Latest review limitation:This review retrieved two 8 October Interfax press-service attributions: contact observation ended and the reported second illness was denied. They are retained as attributed candidates, not final diagnosis, independent safety findings or proof excluding transmission. Key originals remain inaccessible: REVIEW_INCOMPLETE. Accepted claims, evidence-state cutoff and evidence-updated timestamp are unchanged.

Citable stop rule

Do not convert a suspected-event headline into a diagnosis.

The defensible stopping point is: death reported; pneumonia of unknown origin reported; causative agent and plague diagnosis not established. New qualifying evidence must append a new review and state version rather than rewriting this snapshot.
