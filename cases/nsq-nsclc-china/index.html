Clinical context first

Different pathways answer different questions.

These rows do not describe interchangeable options for one patient. Resectability, stage, molecular findings, prior treatment, and clinical fitness change the relevant evidence.

Resectable early or selected locally advanced NSCLCSURGERY EVIDENCE

Chinese lung-cancer guidance describes stage-specific surgery. A published Chinese multicenter cohort reports outcomes after surgery for stage IA–IIIA NSCLC; it is an operated cohort, not a comparison with advanced post-TKI drug patients. National Health Commission guidance · Surgical cohort.

Local treatment / radiotherapySTAGE-DEPENDENT

Record local-treatment eligibility and outcomes separately by stage and intent. The case has not assembled a complete radiotherapy cohort. Chinese guidance.

EGFR-mutated, locally advanced or metastatic, after EGFR-TKI progressionTRIAL RESULT

China approved ivonescimab with pemetrexed and carboplatin for this defined indication in 2024. In the 322-person randomized HARMONi-A trial, final median overall survival was 16.8 months with ivonescimab plus chemotherapy and 14.1 months with chemotherapy control; HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.58–0.95). These are trial-group results, not an individual forecast. NMPA approval · 2026 final OS report · Challenge the bounded regulatory claim.

Lung transplantationEXCEPTIONAL CONTEXT

International candidate guidance limits consideration for cancer to very selected lung-limited, low-invasive adenocarcinoma patterns. It does not establish transplantation as an alternative for the advanced post-TKI trial cohort. ISHLT selection consensus.

ECMO during critical illness or a procedureLIFE SUPPORT

ECMO supports circulation or oxygenation; it does not treat the tumor. A Chinese study counted 2,073 ECMO cases in 1,700 tertiary hospitals in 2018, but its authors could not resolve outcomes for specific diseases from those records. These are not non-squamous NSCLC success figures. Chinese ECMO study · ELSO guidance.

One directly randomized comparison

What the drug trial measures.

HARMONi-A recruited adults with EGFR-variant, non-squamous, locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC after EGFR-TKI treatment at 55 Chinese sites. The 2026 final report used an April 2025 data cutoff.

OVERALL SURVIVAL

16.8 vs 14.1 months

Median overall survival for ivonescimab plus chemotherapy versus placebo plus chemotherapy in this trial. Median follow-up: 32.5 months. Read the final report.

SAFETY

67.1% vs 54.7%

Grade 3 or higher treatment-emergent adverse events in the same groups. The outcome and adverse-event definitions are distinct. Read the final report.

APPLICABILITY

Still a clinical question

The trial does not establish that its median applies to a particular person, another treatment line, surgery, transplantation, or ECMO. Understand population statistics.

Hospital-controlled analysis

Exposure → follow-up → outcome

Within a defined non-squamous NSCLC cohort: stage, EGFR status, prior TKI, treatment line, regimen, relative treatment dates, dose changes, discontinuations, adverse events, overall survival from a specified start date, censoring, and completeness of follow-up.

Authorized deliverable

Aggregate results with methods

Eligibility and exclusions, denominator, treatment patterns, Kaplan–Meier curves with numbers at risk, time-point estimates and uncertainty, missingness, competing care, data provenance, and limits on publication. No patient-level timeline is requested for the public case.

Data status at v0.1: NOT ACQUIRED. Availability, price, analysis rights, publication rights, and ethics/privacy review require a documented written response from the data holder. Silence or refusal is recorded as an access outcome, not as proof that the treatment has no effect.

Stop rule

A group outcome is not a personal success rate.

If asked “What is my probability of success with surgery, a drug, transplant or ECMO?”, this case stops: the pathways have different eligibility and endpoints. It can show each source's population, outcome and uncertainty, then direct the person to a treating multidisciplinary team for clinical interpretation and shared decision-making.
