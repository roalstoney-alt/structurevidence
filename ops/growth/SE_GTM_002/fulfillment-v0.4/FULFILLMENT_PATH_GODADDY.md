## Official obligation

The FTC published the final Decision and Order on 21 May 2025. The Order is
effective on publication and normally continues for 20 years. It requires
GoDaddy Inc. and GoDaddy.com, LLC to maintain a comprehensive information
security program for covered hosting services and information.

The Order requires an initial assessment followed by biennial assessments from
a qualified, objective and independent third-party professional. For each
assessment, GoDaddy must give the FTC Associate Director for Enforcement the
proposed assessor's name, affiliation and qualifications; the FTC may approve
the assessor.

The initial reporting period covers the first 12 months after issuance:
21 May 2025 through 21 May 2026. The assessment must be completed within 90
days after that period ended, giving an outer-bound completion date of
19 August 2026, unless the FTC directed otherwise in writing. The completed
initial assessment must be submitted within 10 days after actual completion.
Subsequent assessment periods are biennial for the duration of the Order.

The assessment must evaluate implementation and effectiveness of the program,
identify gaps or material noncompliance, address earlier findings, and identify
the specific documents, tests, samples and interviews supporting its result.
The assessor cannot rely primarily on management assertions.

## Publicly observable fulfillment path

1. GoDaddy designates a qualified employee responsible for the security
   program and reports it through the board, a committee or responsible senior
   officer.
2. GoDaddy implements, tests and updates the mandated safeguards.
3. GoDaddy proposes a qualified independent assessor to the FTC.
4. The FTC may approve that assessor.
5. GoDaddy gives the assessor relevant information, network and asset scope,
   and material facts.
6. The assessor completes and signs the evidence-based assessment.
7. GoDaddy submits the initial assessment; later biennial assessments are
   retained and supplied to the FTC on request.
8. A responsible senior executive separately certifies compliance annually.

## What public evidence establishes

- The legal obligation and assessor-independence requirements are explicit.
- The initial assessment period and outer-bound completion window have passed
  as of this research date.
- GoDaddy's 2025 annual report and 2026 quarterly filings say it has invested
  and continues to implement the Order's requirements.
- Those filings do not name an assessor or describe an open assessor selection.

## What is unavailable within the public boundary

- proposed or approved assessor identity;
- contract award, scope, value or duration;
- RFP, procurement portal entry or subcontracting notice;
- whether the initial assessment was completed or submitted on a non-public
  date different from the outer bound;
- whether the incumbent needs ancillary support;
- a purpose-fit route to the accountable program owner;
- any statement by GoDaddy confirming demand for StructEvidence.

The Order requires GoDaddy to provide the assessor's identity to the FTC. It
does not require that identity, the assessment or the procurement record be
published. Absence from the reviewed public record therefore cannot establish
that no assessor exists.
