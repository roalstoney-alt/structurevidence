## Two-sided relationship

| Actor | Economic role | Decision or burden | Potential StructEvidence contribution | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FDE provider | Potential paying customer | Must scope, build, evidence and defend a deployment | Reusable evidence core plus customer-specific decision-clearance pack | Willingness to pay is not established. |
| Enterprise customer | Ultimate beneficiary and approval authority | Must decide whether the PoC or deployment satisfies technical, security, governance and business conditions | Transparent claims, provenance, tests, unknowns and stop-points | Customer approval cannot be inferred from provider evidence. |
| Customer engineering / platform owner | Technical verifier | Reviews architecture, integrations, reliability, rollback and operations | Versioned technical evidence and environment-specific validity conditions | Generic evidence cannot replace customer-environment tests. |
| Customer security / GRC | Control verifier | Reviews data access, permissions, logging, model risk and policy fit | Control evidence map and unresolved requirements | StructEvidence is not a certification or accreditation body. |
| Business process owner | Outcome owner | Determines whether the workflow produces usable value | Metric definitions, provenance and before/after evidence | ROI remains customer-specific and must not be manufactured. |
| Procurement / legal | Commercial gate | Reviews terms, data handling, third parties and supplier risk | Traceable source and responsibility record | Contract approval remains outside StructEvidence authority. |
| FDE delivery lead | Pack owner candidate | Coordinates evidence from engineering and customer teams | Maintains bounded reusable core and customer overlay | Role must be confirmed in a real engagement. |

## Relationship finding

The proposed paying-customer relationship is coherent: an FDE provider could
fund a reusable evidence core because it repeatedly bears preparation and
clarification work, while the enterprise buyer retains the approval decision.
However, the reviewed public record does not establish that the provider—not
the enterprise customer, model vendor or systems integrator—owns the relevant
budget.
