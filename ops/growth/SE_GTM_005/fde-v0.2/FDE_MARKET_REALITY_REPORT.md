## Executive finding

Forward Deployed Engineering is a real and increasingly explicit enterprise
delivery model. First-party records from Palantir, OpenAI and AWS consistently
place the FDE inside the customer lifecycle from discovery and technical
scoping through production deployment. They also show that security,
governance, evaluation, controls and measurable business outcomes are part of
the work rather than optional post-deployment additions.

That establishes a plausible verification-cost problem. It does **not**
establish that any reviewed provider is currently buying, or intends to buy,
independent evidence verification. No reviewed source published comparable
hours, clarification-round counts or acceptance-pack costs. The commercial
hypothesis therefore remains unverified.

## Directly observed engagement lifecycle

| Lifecycle stage | Direct first-party observation | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opportunity discovery | OpenAI says FDEs work with business leaders, operators and frontline teams to identify high-value workflows. | Does not establish how long discovery takes or whether evidence work causes delay. |
| Technical scoping | OpenAI's FDE role owns discovery, technical scoping, system design, build and rollout. Entrans describes technical discovery, requirements engineering and bounded requirements. | Does not establish repeated questionnaire volume. |
| Build and integration | OpenAI, AWS and independent providers describe embedding with customer engineering teams and connecting models to customer data, tools and controls. | Marketing descriptions are not independent delivery audits. |
| Evaluation and controls | OpenAI names eval-driven feedback and GRC/security collaboration. Entrans explicitly describes model evaluation, security validation and audit logging. Atlassian describes permissions, guardrails and audit trails. | These sources establish work categories, not a customer acceptance outcome. |
| Production decision | AWS says FDEs design, build, deploy and run production systems with customer business, engineering and security teams. | No common public acceptance standard was identified. |
| Handover and reuse | Several independents promise runbooks, documentation, training, reusable patterns or knowledge transfer. | Reuse across different customer policies and environments remains conditional. |
| Outcome review | OpenAI, AWS, Forward Labs, Deployment Inc and The Tech Team describe measurable outcomes. | No reviewed source independently validates the providers' outcome claims. |

The public sites verify service identities and business routes, not legal-entity
registrations, employee counts or revenue. The similar `fde.team` and
`fdeteam.com` brands were kept as separate reference candidates because their
public relationship is not established; no affiliation was inferred.

## Problem classification

### Directly observed problem categories

- **Security and governance evidence.** AWS describes work with customer
  security teams, data and governance. Atlassian says production agents need
  permissions, guardrails and audit trails. Entrans says stalled pilots can
  encounter security review, SSO and data-access gates.
- **Model and system evaluation.** OpenAI's role description uses eval-driven
  feedback; Entrans and several independents explicitly offer model evaluation,
  evals, logging and observability.
- **Data provenance and operating context.** Public FDE descriptions repeatedly
  place deployments inside customer data, tools, access controls and workflows.
  This makes provenance and validity conditions relevant to acceptance.
- **Business-outcome evidence.** OpenAI describes measurable workflow impact;
  AWS describes measurable customer outcomes; several independents sell against
  production or board-level outcomes.
- **Pilot-to-production gates.** Forward Labs, Entrans, fd.engineering and other
  providers explicitly describe pilots stalling before production because of
  data, permissions, governance, integration or reliability conditions.

### Inferred, not directly established in this review

- repeated customer questionnaires as a quantified engineering cost;
- formal PoC acceptance disputes;
- a standard number of clarification rounds;
- a causal link between evidence preparation and overall sales-cycle length;
- willingness to pay StructEvidence rather than using internal delivery,
  security, GRC or evaluation capabilities;
- reduced customer acquisition cost or faster close rates.
